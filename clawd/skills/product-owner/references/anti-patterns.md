# Product Owner Anti-Patterns

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### ❌ The Requirements Printer
**Pattern:** Acting as a pass-through for stakeholder requests, writing requirements without questioning value.

**Why it's bad:** You're accountable for maximizing value, not just documenting what people ask for.

**Instead:** Challenge every request with "How does this connect to our Product Goal?" and "What evidence do we have that users need this?"

---

### ❌ Everything is High Priority
**Pattern:** Saying yes to everything, avoiding prioritization decisions, treating all items as equally urgent.

**Why it's bad:** If everything is priority, nothing is priority. The team can't focus.

**Instead:** Force-rank items. Use the prioritization formula. Make the hard calls — that's your job.

---

### ❌ The Absent Discoverer
**Pattern:** Going 3+ weeks without talking to customers. Relying on secondhand information or assumptions.

**Why it's bad:** You lose touch with real user needs. Backlog becomes assumption-driven.

**Instead:** Minimum one customer interview per week. Schedule it like any other meeting.

---

### ❌ Opinion-Based Prioritization
**Pattern:** Ordering backlog based on gut feel, loudest stakeholder, or HiPPO (Highest Paid Person's Opinion).

**Why it's bad:** Leads to building the wrong things. Erodes trust when products fail.

**Instead:** "Show me the evidence." Customer quotes, usage data, test results.

---

### ❌ The Feature Graveyard
**Pattern:** Letting items pile up in the backlog without ordering. Backlog grows to hundreds of items.

**Why it's bad:** Creates false expectations. Wastes time discussing things that will never be built.

**Instead:** Keep backlog lean. Top 5-10 items should always be clear. Regularly prune items that don't connect to the Product Goal.

---

### ❌ Building Before Testing
**Pattern:** Jumping straight to development without validating assumptions first.

**Why it's bad:** Expensive way to learn. Sunk cost fallacy kicks in.

**Instead:** Test cheaply first. Fake door tests, prototypes, concierge MVPs. Build only after validation.

---

### ❌ The Lone Wolf
**Pattern:** Making all decisions in isolation. Not collaborating with team or stakeholders.

**Why it's bad:** Misses technical constraints, UX considerations, business context.

**Instead:** Involve developers early. Partner with design. Keep stakeholders informed.

---

### ❌ Scope Creep Enabler
**Pattern:** Accepting endless additions mid-sprint. "Can we just add one more thing?"

**Why it's bad:** Destroys team predictability. Nothing ever ships.

**Instead:** Protect the sprint. New requests go to backlog for prioritization.

---

### ❌ The Micromanager
**Pattern:** Dictating how solutions should be implemented. Over-specifying requirements.

**Why it's bad:** Developers are experts in how. You're expert in what and why.

**Instead:** Define the problem and acceptance criteria. Let the team figure out the solution.

---

### ❌ Success Theater
**Pattern:** Declaring victory without measuring outcomes. Shipping features and moving on.

**Why it's bad:** No learning. No idea if you actually delivered value.

**Instead:** Define success metrics upfront. Measure after release. Share learnings.

---

## Warning Signs

You might be falling into anti-patterns if:

- [ ] You can't remember the last customer you talked to
- [ ] Your backlog has more than 50 items
- [ ] Stakeholders routinely override your prioritization
- [ ] The team asks "why are we building this?" and you can't answer
- [ ] You've shipped features but don't know if they worked
- [ ] Sprint scope changes frequently mid-sprint
- [ ] You spend more time in meetings than with customers
- [ ] You can't explain how current work connects to the Product Goal

## Recovery Actions

If you recognize these patterns:

1. **Schedule 3 customer interviews this week** — break the discovery gap
2. **Prune the backlog** — archive anything not connected to Product Goal
3. **Calculate priority scores** — make ordering evidence-based
4. **Define one clear outcome metric** — know what success looks like
5. **Block discovery time** — treat it as non-negotiable
